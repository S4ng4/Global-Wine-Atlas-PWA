Effervescence Around the World

Sparkling Wine Guide

Understanding the three great winemaking traditions behind fine bubbles — from chalk-aged Champagne and Franciacorta to Spanish Corpinnat, Tasmanian Brut, and vibrant Pét-Nat.

Méthode Traditionnelle / Metodo Classico

Traditional Method

Second fermentation takes place inside the very bottle served at your table. Extended lees aging (18 to 84+ months) imparts fine pinpoint mousse, toasted brioche, hazelnut, and remarkable aging longevity.

Global Icons: Champagne (France), Franciacorta & TrentoDOC (Italy), Cava & Corpinnat (Spain), Méthode Cap Classique (South Africa), Tasmania (Australia), Sonoma Coast (USA).
Charmat / Metodo Martinotti

Tank Method

Second fermentation occurs in pressurized stainless-steel autoclaves, preserving luminous primary fruit aromatics — white peach, pear blossom, and crisp citrus — with an exuberant, refreshing sparkle.

Global Icons: Prosecco Superiore Valdobbiadene (Veneto, Italy), artisanal dry Lambrusco (Emilia-Romagna), crisp Sekt (Germany & Austria).
Méthode Ancestrale / Pét-Nat

Ancestral Method

The oldest sparkling method in history: wine is bottled before primary fermentation finishes, trapping natural carbonation with zero dosage. Often unfiltered, wild, dry, and irresistibly joyful.

Global Icons: Loire Valley & Jura (France), Col Fondo (Veneto, Italy), Penedès Ancestral (Spain), Willamette & Finger Lakes (USA).
